Meet your Dot

What is a Dot?

Your security stack, tested from the inside.

A Dot is a lightweight program on one endpoint. It runs safe, attacker-style tests against the controls you already pay for, fixes routine gaps on its own and reports to VEX, our AI SOC. No heavyweight agent and no console to watch.

  1. Validates your controls

    A safe, controlled test shows whether your EDR and antivirus actually block a threat, not just whether they’re running.

  2. Remediates the routine

    Missing patches and insecure settings are fixed automatically.

  3. Approval for the rest

    Changes that could affect users wait for a person to approve them.

  4. Escalates with context

    What it can’t fix goes to VEX and our security team, findings attached.

Questions

Questions security teams ask.

What data does a Dot collect?

Security state: patch levels, security settings and the results of its own tests. It isn’t there to read files or messages, and we never sell data.

Will it conflict with our EDR?

No. Dots run alongside your EDR and antivirus, and test that they’re doing their job.

What’s the performance impact?

Dots are tiny by design and built to stay out of the way. Most users never notice one is there.

How do Dots relate to VEX?

Every Dot reports to VEX, our AI SOC. One Dot protects one thing; VEX connects what all your Dots find, maps the attack paths between them and coordinates the response. See how VEX works (opens in a new tab).

How do we deploy it?

After sign-up we email install instructions. Dots run on Mac and Windows and take a couple of minutes per machine.

What’s in the monthly report?

What was tested, what was remediated and what needs a decision, per endpoint.

How does pricing work across many machines?

Each endpoint gets its own Dot at 99¢ a month, billed yearly. Planning a larger rollout? Send us a message.

Can we remove it?

Yes, anytime. Remove it like any other app, or send us a message and we’ll walk you through it.

I’m a security researcher. Can we work together?

We’d love to. For more on how Dots work, read our research (opens in a new tab). Then let us know what you have in mind.
