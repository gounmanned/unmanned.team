How VEX works

The VEX loop.

VEX runs every piece of evidence through the same four stages: collect, triage, detect, update. Then it does it again.

Findings and human input become context for future investigations. When new information changes the picture, VEX can reassess.

    Triage signal
    Assess the affected asset and investigate the signal.

  3. 03

    Detect evil
    Determine whether activity is malicious, and challenge the conclusion.

  4. 04

    Update state
    Carry findings and human judgment into the next investigation.

The loop: Triage

Protect the asset. Investigate the signal.

Every signal is triaged as it arrives. If a breach looks likely, VEX can contain the account or device before the investigation is finished, so an attacker’s next move is already limited.

  • 01

    Protect the asset first.

    When a breach looks likely, VEX can contain the account or device. A person may need to release it.

  • 02

    Pull the history.

    VEX looks back through past activity, because a single event rarely tells the whole story.

  • 03

    Close what’s routine.

    If the evidence shows nothing malicious, the signal closes itself. No one has to look at it.

The loop: Detect

Every answer has to survive a challenge.

Most tools wait for an event to match a rule. VEX asks a bigger question: given everything we know, has this business been compromised? Then it tries to prove its own answer wrong.

  • 01

    Ask one focused question.

    An AI analyst gets the evidence, the history and instructions for this one investigation, and answers with its confidence.

  • 02

    Try to prove it wrong.

    Other AI models check the same evidence. Some are told the answer is wrong and have to check for themselves.

  • 03

    Act on the result.

    Agreement raises confidence. Above the threshold, your team gets a signal. Below it, the investigation closes.

The loop: Update

A signal is never final.

A signal isn’t a one-time alert. Whenever something changes, like a new event or a comment from your team, VEX rewrites what it knows and sends the signal through the loop again.

A knowledge store, not a pile of alerts.

  • 01

    Record the decision.

    If VEX finds malicious activity, the signal stays open for a person to investigate. If not, it’s marked auto-closed.

  • 02

    Treat people as evidence.

    A comment or a severity change from your team becomes part of the record, just like an event from an integration.

  • 03

    Run the loop again.

    Every change sends the signal back through collect, triage and detect, with everything VEX now knows.

The loop: Collect

How collection works

Detection starts with reality.

In short

VEX collects activity from everything an attacker might touch, not just endpoints. Data comes in through 100+ integrations, the VEX API, or email for systems without an API. Security-relevant events become signals, and everything else is kept in an audit log that’s never purged.

What VEX collects

To detect malicious behavior, you need a reliable stream of what’s actually happening across an environment. That means collecting activity from everything an attacker might touch:

  • Identities
  • Endpoints
  • Domains
  • SaaS applications
  • Network devices
  • Email systems

The hard-to-reach sources

Identity providers like Microsoft and Google have well-documented APIs and mature integrations. But what about:

  • The firewall?
  • The domain registrar?
  • An ISP portal?
  • DMARC reports?
  • The dozens, or hundreds, of SaaS applications a company relies on?

VEX is designed to make collection boring

  1. Connect an API. Connect any data source through the VEX API.
  2. Push data by email. When a system doesn’t have an API, send its data to the unique mailbox included with every VEX deployment.

VEX supports 100+ third-party integrations, plus an unlimited number of additional data sources that can feed it through the API or email.

Where the data goes

Once connected, VEX continuously collects and normalizes the data.

Security-relevant events
Become signals for analysis.
Everything else
Goes into a never-purged audit log, available for retrieval over the API.

The result is a much broader view of reality than a traditional SOC typically gets: not just what happened on an endpoint, but what happened everywhere.

The loop: Triage

How triage works

Every security signal goes through a real-time, two-stage triage.

In short

Every signal is triaged in real time, in two stages. First the asset: if a breach looks likely, VEX can log the identity out or contain the asset. Then the signal: VEX pulls the history, decides whether there’s evil, and closes the signal itself if there isn’t.

1. Triage the asset

First, VEX looks at the asset associated with the signal (a computer, identity, domain or other resource) and determines whether action should be taken against it.

Log out

Should active sessions be revoked?

Relatively low-impact.

Contain

Should the asset be isolated or otherwise prevented from continuing to operate?

More disruptive. May require a human to intervene before the asset is returned to service.

These actions are reserved for signals with sufficiently high confidence that a breach may be underway.

2. Triage the signal

Next, VEX investigates the signal itself.

  1. Retrieve historical evidence. VEX pulls relevant historical activity to put the signal in context. A single event rarely tells the whole story.
  2. Determine whether there’s evil. VEX analyzes the signal and its surrounding evidence to determine whether it represents malicious activity.
  3. Auto-close when appropriate. If the evidence doesn’t indicate malicious activity, the signal is automatically closed.

This is where collection becomes detection. We’ve gathered the evidence; now VEX has to decide what it means.

The loop: Detect

How detection works

This is where VEX gets opinionated.

In short

VEX doesn’t match events against rules. For each investigation, an AI analyst answers one question: has this environment been compromised? Then models from other families try to prove that answer wrong. Only a conclusion that survives, with enough confidence, opens a signal.

No rules engine

Traditional detection engineering generally starts with a known bad behavior: a suspicious login, an impossible-travel event, a malicious process, a known indicator. You encode that behavior as a rule, wait for an event to match it, and investigate the resulting alert.

VEX doesn’t have a traditional rules engine. There aren’t thousands of detection rules waiting for individual events to match. Instead, VEX gives an AI analyst a bounded set of evidence and asks it to reason about what happened.

Rather than asking

“Does this event match a detection?”

VEX asks

“Given everything we know about this environment, is there high confidence that a compromise has occurred?”

How an investigation runs

Each detection runs as an investigation against a specific question. VEX:

  1. Collects the relevant audit history.
  2. Compresses the most recent hours of audit events and days of security signals into a working state.
  3. Provides that state to the AI analyst along with the detection-specific instructions.

The model returns a decision and its self-reported confidence.

Technical implementation

The runtime context has three layers

  1. Historical context: a vector-based filesystem containing historical signal results and their associated context.
  2. Detection instructions: a system prompt specific to the investigation being performed.
  3. The question: a user prompt containing the single question the analyst must answer.

The model’s decision comes back as structured JSON:

{
  "answer": true,
  "probability": 0.79,
  "response": "A description of what occurred"
}
answer
The decision.
probability
The model’s self-reported confidence.
response
A description of what occurred.

But VEX doesn’t take the model’s word for it

The initial conclusion is independently reviewed by multiple model families. Each reviewer receives the same underlying evidence and is asked to evaluate the conclusion from a different perspective and at different temperatures. Some reviews are deliberately adversarial:

  • “Is the proposed answer correct?”
  • “A reviewer believes the answer is wrong. Check the evidence independently. Is it actually correct?”

The goal isn’t to get several models to agree with one another. It’s to make the original hypothesis survive attempts to disprove it.

How the decision is made

Agreement across independent checks increases confidence. Disagreement introduces uncertainty. VEX combines those evaluations with the analyst’s initial confidence to produce a final confidence estimate: not a binary alert or no alert, but how strongly the evidence supports the hypothesis that the environment has been compromised.

Crosses the detection threshold
VEX creates a signal for investigation.
Doesn’t cross it
The investigation is closed automatically.

The important part isn’t that an AI makes the decision. It’s that the decision is treated as a hypothesis that must survive independent attempts to disprove it.

The loop: Update

How updates work

The loop doesn’t end at a decision.

In short

A signal is never final. Whenever its state changes, including a comment from your team, VEX rewrites its summary and runs it back through the loop. Over time that builds a security knowledge store instead of a pile of static alerts.

Writing back the decision

Once VEX has made a detection decision, it writes that decision back to the signal and persists the updated state.

Malicious activity
The signal remains open for human investigation.
Evidence doesn’t support the hypothesis
VEX marks it auto-closed.

A living piece of security state

A signal’s state can change as new evidence becomes available:

  • Status
  • Severity
  • Disposition
  • Analyst comments

Whenever that state changes, VEX regenerates the signal’s compact stub and sends the signal back through the same collection, triage and detection process.

Compact stub
A current, plain-text summary of what VEX knows about the signal.

People count as evidence

A human analyst’s judgment becomes part of the record’s future context, just like an event collected from an API.

  • A comment can add evidence.
  • A disposition can change the interpretation of an incident.
  • A severity change can alter how the signal is treated.

Each update gives VEX another chance to reconsider what it knows.

The result is a continuously evolving security knowledge store rather than a collection of static alerts.

Your data stays yours

VEX is a multi-tenant system that separates each account into its own logical partition, and runs every query, deterministic or LLM-based, against only the data in that account.
